## Looking back

I don't know what to make of this summer. I didn't do enough, and spent more of it trying things and hesitating. I heard a lot of people's stories and met an older student who'd given up recommended grad admission to stake everything on the entrance exam. I also met one who'd already gotten in through recommended admission in a different field (STAT -> AI), and plenty of people giving it their all in the summer between junior and senior year. The more people I met, the less sure I felt of my own choice. I didn't know what the right thing to do was. In the end I made up my mind in the small hours one morning. If you're reading this, I hope you can make a choice too. I don't know a thing about your options, but the best advice I have is to take the path where you can become who you want to be.

> [!tip] What stuck with me
> The main thing I learned was not to spend forever thinking about it. You only find out how complicated something is by doing it. Try not to start too many things either. See one through and do it well before picking up the next. For example, if you're reading a paper, don't decide to start high-dimensional statistics, high-dimensional probability, random matrices, and measure theory all at once. That's an easy way to overflow the stack.
>
> I also got much faster on the computer after improving my toolchain and getting comfortable with it. With all the shortcuts I barely need a mouse. I learned a few things about keeping up with research, too. I'm definitely first-rate at searching: I find sources and information quickly. My coding may not be there yet, though. Sometimes I need to read code to make better sense of a paper's theory, and I'm not all that fluent at doing that. I also don't have as deep a feel for research directions as some of the older students. 🤔
>
> I might put some more thoughts in next week's report.

## Passing it on

A little memento of playing Monument Valley 3:

> I'd heard of and played MV1 (Monument Valley, MV) in primary school, but I didn't have much to work with then, no Steam account and no internet access. This made up for what I'd missed. The art and the depth of the story are still as good as ever. I was floored, and had a blast playing it! Afterward I realized that for me now the puzzles are no longer the hardest part. The memories I had as a kid will give way to newer ones. Putting this here is a way of settling things with my younger self.
