# Sophomore Spring Review

## Intro

I discovered weekly reports this semester as a way to give myself a push. They help me remember what I got done that week and see where my tools let me down. But a week is a short window and the tasks are scattered, which makes it hard to see the semester as a whole. Now that it's summer, I've pulled the weekly reports together to write a semester recap.

This is mostly about the new techniques and tools I tried, the areas I explored, and what I learned. I'm going month by month so I can see what fell through the cracks over a longer stretch and fix it. The more systematic things, like lab meetings and what I've learned about emailing professors, will probably get their own write-ups in the research section.

## Month by month

### Month 1

First I **got a rough sense of each professor's personality and teaching style**. That more or less set the tone for the semester: **self-study**. My mathematical statistics professor talked a lot without covering much, and my real analysis professor just copied out of the book without offering an understanding of their own. Both courses mattered too much to leave it at that, so I decided to put in extra work outside class and make sure I learned them properly. I also started sitting in on stochastic processes. It wasn't measure-theoretic and the material was fairly simple, but the professor taught it well. I sent my first email to a professor to ask for advice.

> [!tip] Getting ready for the semester
> I've learned what to expect at the beginning of a term. Most people spend the entire break having fun, so they come back and study furiously to make up for it for about a month. Don't panic. Everything goes back to normal a month in.

Then I did some work on my blog. I gave it a facelift with the front-end trio, HTML+CSS+JS. After trying Jupyter Notebook I decided to use it for code notes, including simple demos and plots. NB is good for debugging, and I could pair it with the MkDocs code plugin.

I finished the first season of *Peaky Blinders* and started getting hooked on British TV.

#### New tools

- [x] SimpleTeX
- [x] PicGo+Typora+Cloudflare image hosting
- [x] LaTeX notes template
- [x] Beamer

#### New areas

- [x] Econometrics (undergraduate innovation project)
- [x] Machine learning (Andrew Ng, Hung-yi Lee)
- [x] Deep learning (Mu Li)

What I took from it: I don't need a ton of plans. I need to review what I did more often, make it more efficient, and reflect on it. I don't need to expect too much from research or from myself. A rough direction and some progress are good enough. I'll probably start a separate section later on how I use each little tool. My theory work still doesn't go deep enough, but it takes time, and I need to rest more too.

### Month 2

I changed the site's domain, got study material from some friends and older students, and started preparing for midterms. Then I bombed the monthly exams. For real analysis I finally went ALL IN on Folland. Operations research left me baffled: the theory was stressful but the problems were mechanical, which set me up for a nasty surprise on the final. My coding didn't improve much, and I started doing 61A exercises.

I went back to shoring up my mathematical statistics and filled in some basic ideas and views (mbq is amazing. The math foundations at USTC are solid).

I came across a number of good open courses from abroad. See my Qingming Festival weekly report. They were pretty good, and I started to feel like I was making something of my time:

- [x] Mu Li: Dive into Deep Learning
- [x] Mu Li: CS329P
- [x] Chen Dayue: Applied Stochastic Processes
- [x] UCB-CS61A (a turning point on my second try)

### Month 3

I went to Beijing for the **GBC pop-up**. It was awesome and made me very happy. I updated some of my methods, had a talk with a professor, and took some theory from machine learning and applied it to my day-to-day studies. But I got sick, probably from allergic rhinitis, and spent a long time trying to get better.

### Month 4

I looked into building a PC, got some books from an older student, and tried to recreate Ob's LaTeX Suite plugin in VSC. Then I added Copilot. I also came across some good resources, such as *On Practice*, *Adachi and Shimamura*, GTM282, and MIT 6.006. Then it was back to boring revision.

Overall, there was too much I wanted to do and too little I saw all the way through. I could use some more staying power and discipline. I need to bring those scraps of time together and use them to finish one thing!

## Looking back

The pressure this semester was more than I'd imagined. So was the amount of information I came across. I gained a lot of experience and ideas and did some actual thinking about the future. It feels like I'm standing at the entrance to a treasure house. I hope I get stronger and better at focusing in the future.
