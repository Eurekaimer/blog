### Recalling the written exam content

I can recall a few problems a little. The problems and solutions are all organized in the recollection document <a href="/blog/assets/ustc_management_written_exam.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">2026-USTC-School of Management-Written Exam</a>; you can skip this part and go straight to the document

My impression after finishing at the time was roughly: the first two problems were basically free points. The first was a proof of a limit and a series convergence, namely $\lim\limits_{n \to \infty} \sum\limits_{k=1}^{n} \frac{n^{k}}{k!}e^{-n}$ and $\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n} \left( \frac{1}{k}-\ln\left( 1+ \frac{1}{k} \right) \right)$; the second was a discussion of the nullity of a matrix with parameters and the dimension of the linear space it generates (that must be because I did not remember the matrix, but this problem is obvious); then there was proving $tr(A^{-1})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} \frac{1}{a_{kk}-\alpha_{k}^{T}A_{k}^{-1}\beta_{k}}$, which is easy to do using the Schur inverse (I also got it by expanding according to the definition at the time, but later I thought about it and direct construction is fine); there was also a problem where, given $\gamma$, $X_{1},X_{2}$ both follow an exponential distribution with parameter $\gamma$, while $\gamma$ follows $\Gamma(\alpha,\lambda)$, and you had to find the distributions of $X_{1},X_{2}$ and $F(X_{1},X_{2})$; the second part asked for the joint distribution of $F_{i}(X_{i})$ and a probability limit $\lim\limits_{u \to 1^{-}} Pr(F_{1}(x_{1})>u|F_{2}(x_{2})>u)$ (about that probability limit, I later thought about it and must have calculated it wrong, a bit foolish); the last problem gave the time series recurrence $X_{t+1}=\phi X_{t}+\varepsilon_{t},\varepsilon_{t}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})$ and $Y_{t}=\phi_{1}Y_{t-1}+\phi_{2}Y_{t+1}+\delta_{t},\delta_{t}\sim N(0,\sigma^{2})$, and then the questions were all about finding the MLE of $\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\sigma^{2}$.

> Later edit: in the end I did get this offer. Thank you very much to the teachers for being willing to tolerate this noob; it also fulfilled a fixation from my failed Strong Foundation Plan attempt in high school

Some extra experience about learning a thing, derived from the interview failure:

+ If it is an algorithm, you had better hand-roll it once yourself (moderate wheel-reinventing is necessary)
	+ Many people criticize reinventing wheels as unnecessary, but I think it is still useful; it really can make you a bit stronger
+ If it is a concept, you had better assume an examiner is grilling you and asking follow-up questions step by step
	+ The method of follow-up questioning is also an art
+ If it is an open-ended question, you can try answering in a short time (compressing your thinking time), then give a result after thinking for a long time. This is a way to speed up your reactions (my interview reactions are still a bit too poor, not adaptable enough)

## How do you want to live your life

It is now 2026.6.8, Monday. How do you want to live your life?

I have not written this diary for a long time. It is now roughly the end of the final exams of the second half of my third year, and I feel utterly despondent. I bombed an exam in a very simple course; the thing I hate happened to be on it, and even after reviewing I did not do it well. Misery.

One course gave me full marks on the paper (100/100), probably the first time, and the final grade was full marks too. It felt like completing an achievement

Why are all the code-related courses offered by the statistics school so boring? And they are all very ancient things and code memorization. Who in their right mind would memorize this stuff? Would not flipping through the manual or asking AI be faster than thinking it up myself? Is research also closed-book with no documentation allowed...

Today all the grades came out, and I am probably just about on the recommendation line. Including the earlier study-abroad option, my recommendation should be secured, but so what? I started thinking about what I have done in these three years: actually I have not made many friends, but I think with too many friends I would not have the energy to maintain them either, so it is probably just right; as for romance, I roughly decided on celibacy at the end of my first year — bringing a person into this world to suffer is obviously a cruel decision, so not marrying and not having children is natural; as for studies and research, I worked hard in my first year and found that as long as I go all out I rank near the top, but is maintaining that high-pressure state really the destination of life? At the end of my first year I came across the [CSDIY](https://csdiy.wiki/) website, and then began a frenzied study of the computer world. I feel those foreign open courses are what truly taught me knowledge, so I actually do not value the school's GPA and courses that much anymore,,, I roughly just want to stay within the recommendation line. Then there is research: my original idea was roughly to experience it, and if the pressure was too much I would run; in the end I did not persist either, and it even affected my mental state somewhat. I feel the negative effects are greater,,, is something disconnected from industry and disconnected from reality really the research I originally idealized? I think not? **I am going to chase my own North Star**, that is, CS/Distributed Systems. But after stepping out I found that this kind of disconnection is actually universal: industry is still in a ramshackle state, and a more advanced kind of ramshackle state, where people's hearts are even more complicated.

No matter what, I no longer have the energy to review probability and statistics or attend other summer camps. I have decided to just take that USTC offer in the end. Tsinghua and Peking are out of reach, and among the Hua Five the platform difference will not be huge, not to mention that my destination is the internet industry, which does not look at educational background. I am preparing to begin a three-month lying-flat path, maybe two and a half months (one Kun-month). There should be that line from *Hyouka* here; I vaguely remember Oreki's sister saying it: "Then give yourself a long holiday." I think I should take a holiday too, and socializing probably will not happen either; I do not have the strength for it...

Summer internships are not easy to find. I feel I lack project experience and relevant basic knowledge, and even interview trivia I learned stumblingly. I started reading some 408-related content, but I feel what is written in the books is still very ancient and somewhat out of touch with actual industrial production. In short, this 408 reeks of liberal arts; I do not know who is saying this is a science...

> Resumed communication with some people, but I feel we are not really on the same frequency and cannot talk well, so in the end I just gave up on socializing altogether
