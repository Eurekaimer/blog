## The shifting of technology

Speaking of technology, even people who know nothing about CS/DS bring up AI (Artificial Intelligence). To me the word has already become a bit restless and imprecise, like the overuse of decorators in Python: you have to combine it with some other field, otherwise that field is apparently devalued

Because this field (rather than AI, you might as well just call it LLM) is so important in today's society, products are constantly compared, just like the newest fandoms: "Hakimi is number one in the world," "DeepSeek is my daddy," "GPT crushes everything," "Claude mows down code," and so on — such talk is already everywhere. The sparring is not only within the industry either; countries' ability to develop their own large models is often compared too. The most classic cases are DeepSeek annihilating everything, or the American empire's closed-source large models wiping the floor with domestic ones. Below I want to write down some of my observations and thoughts:

### The rise of DeepSeek

DeepSeek at the start of the year (or last year, but it became widely known early this year) was undoubtedly a shot in the arm for domestic large models: extremely low cost, excellent results — you could call it a revolutionary achievement (zero to one). Its performance, at least right when R1 launched, was absolutely SOTA level. You have to acknowledge its "place in history"

### The three giants rising in turn

Then came the big closed-source/open-source models from the American empire, taking turns on stage: GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Grok 4, Sora 2, Claude (the moderation on this one is stricter, so I have not used it) —

Personally I like Gemini 2.5 a bit more; for handling math problems it is the best in my book

### Gemini 3 takes the top

Then there is the Gemini 3 model released at the end of this year. Just from my own experience using it, it completely blows away every model I used before: almost no hallucinations, it follows requirements well, and you can copy Markdown and LaTeX straight out of it, which is a huge convenience for a statistics mouse who likes to write homework in $\LaTeX$ (it can even help with my research)

What is more impressive is probably its project-building ability (I have used Trae CN, which is decent, but I think it is far from the effect of integrating Gemini 3). I give it one requirement and it can dig out more points on its own, create visualization pages and simple projects. If in the future unlimited tokens could be integrated into an AI IDE that is convenient to use in China, I think it would be extremely powerful (in my dreams — freeloading still is not as good as paying)

> [!tip] A small hope for domestic models
> I hope someone develops an AI IDE as powerful as Cursor, a code LLM as powerful as Claude, and an LLM as powerful as Gemini 3 for writing and helping with research

## Maintaining relationships

As I get older, I find that if I am a star of high density, the people around me are more like asteroids that occasionally pass through my orbit and cannot be captured. Right now the friends I can genuinely call harmonious number fewer than ten, probably? But I think this is quite reasonable; otherwise socializing would definitely affect my normal life.

>This number is supported by the social psychology concept of Dunbar's number

But if I start from my daily life and work backward, it is quite natural to reach the conclusion that "this person's day does not need other people's intervention," so I think this is not too bad a thing. Ever since I made my goals clear, my relationships with other people have become extremely simple (co-worker), and this mode of life avoids the expectation-gap effect very well, turning life directly into minimalism. And I do not plan to find a partner, so there should be no financial pressure or pressure from the other person's family; I only need to push my own life and work forward. I think I am currently in a very self-consistent state (Work Life Balance!)

### Stupid exams

I think my biggest lessons about grades are these:

- [x] Exams should be oriented toward problems, not knowledge
- [x] Grades have nothing to do with how well you have learned
- [x] Do not be sad because of grades

From every monthly exam after I finished changing majors in my second year — trembling with fear, always EMO for a long time whenever I did badly, then often giving up treatment midway and curling up in the dorm, and in the end always dying in a relieved sort of way — including not getting ideal grades in some of the most important courses in the second half of my second year (very sad)

Sometimes exams really do not depend much on the results of your studying, but on whether you can accurately grasp the signals the teacher releases before the exam and prepare in advance. For example, reviewing toward the homework and toward past papers is often more effective than reviewing toward knowledge, and correspondingly this also creates a lot of opportunists. For example, I think most people's actual grasp of knowledge should converge to 0 in probability, yet they can score around seventy or eighty. I think this is also the result of the exam system being broken (teachers are unwilling to create new problems, and the vast majority of courses are just reading the PPT/textbook)

> I cannot imagine why so many science and engineering students are about to graduate without even knowing the operational properties of matrices, why they have never opened GitHub, and only use Bilibili to watch those actually simplified book-reading courses and feel they have found a treasure (I still do not understand why some people think certain Bilibili tutorials are good). They do not even want Face anymore! Those courses (frankly, basically all domestic ones) are of quite low quality; the content is either very simple (Copy the book and read it), or completely exam-oriented with no theory at all (no fun)

### How I study

Years of experience have told me that the most suitable learning method for me is to study on my own toward books, because I do not want my learning to be constrained by others (at the very least, the first time I learn something I always learn it myself first, or preview it). Other people's explanations interfere with my thinking. I have an innate nature of doubting everything (many people call it being contrarian), so I usually only treat the teacher's explanation as a reference, then try to disprove or prove it. In this process I complete my learning and iterate out my own thinking. This is also the approach I have worked out since starting college that suits me relatively well, and based on this idea, the structure of my note system has also improved quite a bit over these nearly three years

> [!tip] Copying the book
> Most science and engineering students' notes are basically copying the book. Even if you only delete the narrative sentences and the examples, there is very little streamlined note-taking left; more often it is copied word for word. This method has clearly had its reasons for being passed down so long, but I do not think it is the better algorithm.
> 
> My personal experience also tells me this is already a method with a relatively high lower bound: you quickly familiarize yourself with the content by copying the book, and it is quite natural to gain some insight during the copying. If you can dig deep into a certain point and derive a series of results, and confirm them from different perspectives, that is naturally even better.
> 
> In my view, the reason for copying the book is that you do not yet understand the content being studied well, cannot yet cover it with a systematic theory, and do not know where it sits. So the simplest way to handle it is to follow the textbook's structure, which naturally turns into copying the book — so to avoid copying the book you first have to learn the content you are copying (which seems a bit contradictory), then use your own ideas to organize it. After changing its arrangement you will naturally discover the strengths and weaknesses of the original arrangement, and then you can understand the author's pains (or the fact that the author is an sb).
> 
> The method I consider relatively good is to first follow my own understanding and use the existing framework to optimize the copied book, then after finishing, go back and rework it once, iterate and upgrade, add new content, or when studying later courses go back and supplement the original foundational content (for example, studying real analysis and functional analysis can help with learning mathematical analysis)

What I am relatively satisfied with is actually my real analysis notes. At the time I really did think about a lot of problems, and their maturity became somewhat better. Unfortunately I did not finish them, and in the end they were left sitting in my Obsidian vault unpublished

I also do not know what the point of attending discussion seminars is. The presenter may be very familiar with that content, but the audience sometimes has to endure a truly terrible Lecturer (a role I sometimes play). I also do not know where this tradition came from. If a teacher were involved (or someone who has studied the course), and beginners were made to learn quickly and present, with a group of normal people constantly asking questions, the efficiency might really be okay (the gap is roughly that the time spent playing with your phone while reading on your own and falling into some logical traps could be saved)

> [!warning] Disclaimer
> This cannot be a reason for you to skip your group meeting and go have fun

For everyone who learns knowledge through self-study, I think doing exercises is necessary, because there is no other more convenient way to test the results of your learning, and exercises are another means of learning knowledge. Many authors even hide important conclusions and techniques in the exercises (sometimes there are also open problems to watch out for). Doing exercises is an important way to connect theory with practice

> [!cite] The problem-solving crowd
> Many math students get classified as the problem-solving crowd, mainly because in daily life they treat doing problems as capital for confidence, regard problem-solving as a kind of mental victory method, and look down on everyone. This is classified as the ordinary problem-solving crowd. But there are also extreme idiots who regard doing exercises as a shameful means; most of those who laugh when they see exercise-solving do so because their own level is poor and they have too much time, so they have to waste it on online arguments —

### Getting into anime through Kyoto

I probably entered the two-dimensional world after seeing the delicate animation and full emotional expression in Violet Evergarden and being shaken by it. Then in high school I came across the Hyouka novels, and watched the anime many times (frankly, I think the adaptation is very good). Then around the summer after my first year, a junior recommended that I watch K-On!. After that, researching relationships between girls and director Yamada's life experience and body of work, I dove vertically into real yuri.

> [!tip] Kyoto
> You can see that most of the works I watched early on were created by Kyoto Animation. It successfully spoiled my demand for detailed animation; for some very classic shows I now find it somewhat hard to accept them because of the art style (Attack on Titan, JOJO, etc.)

***Once again, a moment of silence for those who lost their lives in the Kyoto Animation arson attack……***

### Real yuri, starting from Citrus

Citrus should be the first real yuri anime I watched. In terms of story alone it is far inferior to the manga, the animation still has places where it falls apart, and at the beginning it genuinely startled me (no spoilers), but for its historical status I still give it some partiality; it does have some merits and famous lines.

![yuri](https://img.eurekaimer.icu/20261018100051-0004.webp)

Starting from Citrus I watched a lot of quite famous yuri anime (within the industry). My understanding of yuri probably stayed at the level of girls' sweet romances, but the meaning of love in them I still viewed according to traditional views — until a truly great work changed my original view of love

### Thinking about love, starting from Bloom Into You

Bloom Into You will surely be an indelible work in my heart. Whether it is the story, the animation, the voice acting, the core, or the music, it is maxed out. How to understand Nanami Touko, who falls in love with "Koito Yuu, who will not fall in love with anyone"; how to understand Miss Saeki; how to understand Koito Yuu, who dares not admit her own love — these are questions very much worth thinking about —

> [!cite] Favorite works
> Before I encountered 周次, my favorite yuri work was definitely Bloom Into You (the anime) or Iruma's Adachi and Shimamura (the novel), but 周次 is just too delicate! And its distorted feeling and some pathological possessiveness hit my strike zone precisely

### The substance of yuri

You could say yuri is the biggest extra harvest of my college years. For a long time, my only daily diversion was reading those novels and manga. I think for me this is a very good way to dispel negative emotions, but the environment in the community right now is not especially good, so I still have to focus on the works

For me, the entry-level yuri work, if starting from light yuri, should obviously be K-On!. Earlier I did not pay special attention to Bocchi the Rock! (actually, for me, I am not very into Bocchi), then I got into the genre through Citrus, Sakura Trick, Bloom Into You and so on, and finally, because yuri anime was too abstract, I had to move to novels and manga (hanging around 百合会), where I also found good yuri works (an incest yuri written by a certain author). After joining a readers' group I made some friends, and also ran into some abstract people (hard to say, abstract people are everywhere)

## The meaning of life

As that master of the absurd, Camus, said: "Suicide is the only question worth discussing." I have been feeling this more and more lately. Life is the thing I currently have no clue about at all: how does it happen? Why am I me? Why do I have consciousness? I cannot answer any of these, so choosing to end one's own life is obviously worth discussing. People like us are perhaps just moving along some loose constraints on a track that has been drawn for us.

So I often doubt the purpose of this universe —

But I cannot answer —
