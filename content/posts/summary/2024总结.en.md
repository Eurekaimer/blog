# 2024 Year in Review

## Preface

> I also posted this on Zhihu. No, I didn't steal it. Both accounts are mine. Please don't blow my cover, thanks.

I hadn't taken a proper look back at my studies or my life in a while. Finals are almost over, so here I am rushing out a low-effort year-end answer on Zhihu (kidding). I'm not particularly good at any of this. It's just an ordinary year of trying my hardest to pass for a normal college student (please report the p-value, doge). Take it as a little diary, and please don't yell at me for not working hard enough. Much appreciated.

## The year itself

### Background

I'm an ordinary sophomore at a certain C9 university. Since starting freshman year last year (or is it the year before now? It was still last year when I wrote this), I've mostly been scraping through compulsory exams and finding the right moments to catch up on anime, play games, and the like. I didn't study much beyond the usual mathematical analysis, higher algebra, analytic geometry, and so on.

### A short recap

School and life did pick up a bit in 2024. First I pushed through the language debuff and studied from some English textbooks and courses (though looking back, I wouldn't vouch for how solidly I learned any of it). Then I learned to use, or rather tinkered with, some LaTeX and Markdown software. Being able to write simple term papers and class notes in LaTeX might be my second-biggest gain this year. The biggest was probably having enough confidence in my English to try reading some ancient papers and other material, and to look for resources in English-speaking communities. (Technology lights up the world, and it really does feel good when people around the world help one another.)

My interests are fairly broad, so in the first half of 2024 I dipped into a bit of everything (topology, real analysis, probability) and found all of it interesting. I read a little about measure theory and category theory (neither takes much to start). I kept my distance from algebraic geometry, PDEs, and the other eldritch languages that get so much airtime on Zhihu. (I didn't have the prerequisites, didn't want to be someone who just knows the buzzwords, and I'm not good at picking things up on demand.) Then I realized I was particularly interested in probability. Once I'd read enough to see that probability rests on measure theory, something clicked. I decided I'd give the theory a proper go the next year.

Speaking of giving things a proper go, this is probably where I ought to digress.

### The rant

**Warning!!! Strong personal feelings below**

Fuck, why is there so much shit to do at university? And so much of it is pointless and boring! I'm not much for socializing. My hobbies are watching anime, playing games, and at most reading math books. A dumb little rat like me would rather take the sewer when I have to go out. I was genuinely shocked by how much I couldn't get out of, and it even pushed me a little toward being an extrovert. I kept wondering whether other universities had this much nonsense too. Asked around: they're all like that. Ha. Great!

## Back on topic

I do have some thoughts on what it means to study properly. People just entering college (yes, me) probably still think like schoolkids in a lot of ways, or whatever you want to call it. I hardly dared skip class and was always thinking about how to keep my grades up in every course and score as high as possible. I knew perfectly well that none of that was especially good for learning mathematics, and then I just got anxious about everything. The competition around me didn't help. There was always talk pressuring people about recommended grad admission and entrance exams, like it was arson and murder to stop before a master's or PhD. I didn't have much experience reading math books either. I'd get halfway down a page and realize I'd already moved on from something I hadn't understood. I'd go around in circles without getting it, which just added pressure. (Now I think I wasn't used to the language and didn't know what to focus on. I spent too much time verifying proofs and too little thinking about how someone came up with them. In a word, I wasn't using my head.)

This semester felt a lot better. I was selective about what I attended and got a lot out of it. Not that my GPA went up: I watched more anime, made progress on my hours played, and got happier. In ordinary life people are always telling me not to worry, which strikes me as easier said than done. How are you supposed to *not* think about your future? But after slacking off long enough, I actually got somewhere. Things that once looked hazy suddenly read quite naturally. Problems I thought I had no chance of solving began to unravel when I sat and patiently picked at them. That feeling of everything fitting together was what I'd wanted in the first place, not “What's your GPA?” I'm starting to get how to read a math book and to have ideas when I see a problem. It feels great.

Now that I've learned a thing or two, I ought to use it. I finally feel like I can start exploring some deeper mathematics. It feels like those happy, hopeful days are back. Here's hoping I slack off a little less next year.

## Odds and ends

For a change of pace I joined a few math group chats, but the atmosphere in most of them wasn't great. People kept going “mol” at one another. It didn't do much for me, and it wasn't interesting. I'm clearly not the sort of thing people would “mol” over anyway, so I gave it up.

For the less math-related stuff, I added a few younger students to my contacts, I guess? (No underclasswomen, really.) I found that the guys just below me at my old high school were more normal than most college students I'd met (as a shut-in I can't draw a remotely reliable conclusion from the people around me). I helped answer a few of their questions. I also dug up some things I'd meant to try freshman year and got ready to learn them slowly, mostly stuff from CSDIY. I don't plan to switch to programming, but I can't resist things that look this interesting. I built a personal site to play with too, as a little memento.
